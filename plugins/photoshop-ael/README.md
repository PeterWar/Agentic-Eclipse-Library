# Adobe Photoshop connector for Agentic Eclipse Library

Choose the RAW photos of a total solar eclipse, and optionally their darks and flats. AEL stacks them into one image and opens it in Photoshop as a layered 16-bit PSB: the display base and AEL's default filter layers (NRGF, WOW edge-aware and isotropic ACHF 04), or the filters you choose in **Advanced settings…**, each with its blend mode and opacity, ready to adjust.

There is nothing else to set but where the result goes and, if you want, other filters. Everything else is measured on the files and written in a receipt.

For more faithful results and more filters, try the whole Agentic Eclipse Library with an AI agent: <https://github.com/PeterWar/Agentic-Eclipse-Library>. The dialog says so in a box of its own, «Agentic Eclipse Library», with that address written as a web link (blue, with a line under it) that opens the page, and the licence under it.

## Use

1. In Photoshop, choose **File > Scripts > Browse…** and select `AELPhotoshop.jsx`.
2. **Totality RAW photos — Choose…**: select every RAW photo of the totality (all exposures together). Photos of the partial phases, darks or flats among them are recognised and left out.
3. **Darks (optional) — Choose…** and **Flats (optional) — Choose…**: select the dark frames and the flat frames of the same camera, if you have them, each in its own field.
4. The line beside **Advanced settings…** lists the filter layers and their opacities: AEL's three default layers unless you choose others. **Advanced settings…** lists AEL's 16 filters, each with its blend mode and an opacity (rest the pointer on a filter to see what it shows): a ticked filter becomes a layer, visible at its opacity, or added hidden at 0 %, to turn on by hand. **AEL defaults** and **All 16** set the ticks in one click.
5. **Output folder — Browse…**: where the results go. The dialog starts at Downloads, and afterwards at the folder used last time while it exists. Each run makes a new folder inside it, named with the date and time (`AEL_YYYYMMDD_HHMMSS_…`), so nothing is overwritten.
6. Press **Make the image**. Photoshop's own progress window (**Progress**) shows each step as AEL takes it, with a bar and a clock that moves every second. A filter, or writing the PSB, can take minutes: after 20 s on the same step a second line says that AEL is still working on it. Its **Cancel** button stops AEL at the end of the step, and after 15 s at the latest; nothing is opened then.

   Until 31-10 the connector showed its own window, and on Photoshop 2026 for macOS that window was never drawn while AEL worked: it stayed at 0 × 0 pixels, off screen, and the only sign of life was the spinning wheel. Photoshop's progress window is drawn, its bar and its text change, and its Cancel button answers while the script waits.

Every field and button of the dialog says what it does when the pointer rests on it, with the words of the PixInsight connector.

When it finishes, the layered image opens and a message says how many photos were stacked, which were left out and why, and where everything was saved: the new folder, with the PSB, the linear master (32-bit float TIFF in DN per second, no data = NaN), a preview, `AEL receipt.json` and the recipes in `details/`. The message ends with the invitation to the whole library and its address.

PixInsight 1.9.4 reads a floating-point TIFF whose last sample is NaN as all zeros, so this master opens black there. To go on in PixInsight, make the stack with the PixInsight connector: the same photos give the same stack, written for PixInsight.

## One engine for PixInsight and Photoshop

The work from the RAW files to the filter layers is done by one Python file, `python/ael_auto.py`, and the PixInsight connector carries the same file (`tools/check_connectors.py` in the repository fails if the two copies differ). The same photos give the same linear master and the same filter layers in both programs; only the last step differs: Photoshop opens one layered PSB, PixInsight opens the layers as images with the PixelMath expressions that compose them. Both dialogs keep the results the same way: an output folder that starts at Downloads, and then at the folder used last time, with a new folder for each run. `tools/check_connectors.py` also checks that both dialogs ask for the totality RAW photos, the optional darks and the optional flats, and that both offer the same advanced settings, with the same 16 filters and the same words for each, and the same invitation to the whole library.

So far the engine has been tried on the totality photos of two eclipses, 2024 and 2026, taken with five cameras. It is meant for any total eclipse and should work for others, but both connectors still have to be tried on photos of more eclipses.

## What AEL works out by itself

- **Which photos are totality frames.** A photo is a totality frame when AEL finds the dark lunar disc with a bright ring around it, or, in a long exposure whose lunar disc is saturated by scattered light, when its corona registers on the reference. Partial phases are left out. Photos where the photosphere shows (Baily's beads, the diamond ring) are left out too: they saturate beside the lunar limb, within 90° of position angle, at exposures so short that it takes more than twice the brightest light any clean photo shows there. So are photos whose light does not follow the others (a photometric gain beyond 2×, or more than 20 % scatter around the common corona): sunlight scattered near a contact, or a cloud.
- **One pointing.** A mount re-pointed during the totality leaves photos covering another part of the sky; where their edges cross the image the filters would draw that edge. Only the pointing of the reference photo is stacked (a jump of more than 2 % of the sensor's diagonal between consecutive photos starts another pointing; a drift does not), and the other photos are listed as left out.
- **One camera, one ISO.** If photos of several cameras or ISO values are chosen, the camera and ISO with the most photos are used and the others are listed as left out. Each optic needs its own distortion model before images of two optics can be fused, so they are never mixed.
- **Darks and flats.** Each file chosen as a dark or as a flat is still measured from its preview, then checked on its raw data; one that is not what it was chosen as (a flat among the darks, a light frame) is left out, and the receipt says why. Darks are combined by exposure (median, up to 32 per exposure) and each photo gets the dark of its exposure and ISO; a photo without one is calibrated with the black level. Flats are combined with AEL's master flat (each Bayer plane normalised, up to 48 frames).
- **Black and saturation levels.** The black level is the mean level of the masked border of the sensor when the file has one (LibRaw does not know every camera: for the Canon EOS R6 Mark III it declares 0–97 where the sensor reads 512.2), otherwise LibRaw's. The saturation level is the plateau where the sensor clips. The top 15 % of each RAW range is not used, because some sensors stop being linear well before they clip; the shorter exposures cover it.
- **The registration on the Sun.** The corona is fixed on the Sun and the Moon moves across it. Each photo is registered on the corona of a reference photo: a band-pass of the logarithm of the corona between 1.36 and 3 solar radii (saturated pixels, the photo's own Moon and pixels without data left out), first by cross-correlation and then by Gauss-Newton for a shift and a turn of the field about the Sun (a mount that does not follow the sky turns the field during the totality). Two checks: the result must not depend on its starting point, and the coronal structure must match better than the same photo turned by 90° and 270°. A long focal length is registered at a reduced scale, where the Sun is about 240 pixels across its radius. The lunar limb of every photo is measured to about a tenth of a pixel. Over a totality the Moon's motion against the Sun is a straight line in time, so that line is fitted on the photos registered on the corona, the turn of the field is fitted as a straight line in time too, and every photo is placed by its own lunar limb minus the line. A photo that the corona places far from the line is left out. When the Moon and the corona disagree by more than a pixel on that line, or the line is faster than the Moon can cross the Sun (0.65″ a second for a Moon of at least 944″, which is what wrong capture times give), the data cannot tell which is biased: each photo registered on the corona keeps its own registration, and a photo without one stays out, with the reason in the receipt. Without capture times, or with fewer than three photos on the corona, the corona registration is used as it is.
- **One sky for all photos.** Thin clouds or haze can change the light during a totality, and exposure times are not exact. AEL measures one photometric gain per photo where it and the others have good unsaturated data (a coarse grid of radius and position angle around the Sun, through each photo's final placement, fitted by alternating medians with the reference fixed). What the gains leave unexplained is the sky's consistency. Above 3 %, the sky changed: the gains go to the stack (`flux_scale`) and the closing message says so. Under a clear sky the nominal exposure times are kept: there the gain fit carries more bias of its own (the blur of the inner corona, the faint sky of the long exposures) than the shutter carries error. In both cases each photo's weight fades over the top half of its range instead of the top fifth, and the next shorter exposure takes over across that whole half (since AEL 1.4.1 the fading adds to the photo's variance, as much as one photo of the shorter exposure has at its middle): two exposures never agree exactly where the longer one saturates, an abrupt hand-over would leave a step that the filters draw as a ring, and the filters that normalise by the local noise draw a sudden change of grain as a contour. Under thin cloud one gain per photo is not enough (the cloud dims the corona and spreads part of its light around it, by different amounts in different photos): when the sky changed, each photo also gets a smooth level field, a gain and a background against the common stack wherever it is linear, over 64 pixels, with what all photos share over 512 pixels left as it is. The background is taken from each colour in proportion to its light, so the colour is untouched (AEL 1.4.1).
- **Dust and hot pixels.** Without flats, a dust shadow on the sensor repeats in the stack as a row of dark dots when the photos sit apart on the sensor. When the photos of an exposure sit at least 3 px apart, AEL finds the shadows on the photos themselves and hot pixels per exposure with the noise measured around each pixel; their pixels leave every photo and are never replaced: the other photos cover that part of the sky (AEL 1.4.1). With flats, the flat removes them.
- **The Sun.** The Sun's centre is the Moon's centre at the middle of the photos, on the fitted lunar motion. That is exact when the photos span the totality symmetrically and the site is on the central line; elsewhere the true centre is off by up to the difference of the radii, sideways to the Moon's motion. The Sun's radius is the lunar radius over the eclipse's ratio of apparent diameters (a table of total eclipses from 2008 to 2035); when the camera's date matches no eclipse of the table, the lunar radius is used. North, parity and plate scale are not known.
- **Colour.** The camera's daylight white balance and its camera-to-sRGB matrix, derived from the colour matrix LibRaw holds for the camera. The corona keeps the colour that a camera set to daylight records: nothing is neutralised. The matrix is the one of the camera as it left the factory: a camera modified for astronomy (its infrared filter replaced) comes out reddish, and its colour has to be set by hand in Photoshop.
- **The filters.** AEL's default layers with AEL's default opacities, or those chosen in **Advanced settings…**, over a display base scaled on the corona between 1.1 and 2 solar radii. The band that the Moon swept across the corona during the photos enters no filter: there the stack mixes different photos. Since AEL 1.4.1 the NRGF does not draw rings around the Sun where the frame's edge cuts its annuli.

## Requirements

- macOS with Adobe Photoshop 2026 (tested with 27.11.0).
- Python 3.10 or later with these packages, installed once from the Terminal:

```bash
python3 -m pip install numpy scipy opencv-python-headless pillow tifffile imageio psd-tools rawpy exifread
```

  `exifread` is optional: without it, AEL cannot name the camera or read fractions of a second in the capture times.
- The connector finds Python by itself: the Python that worked last time, `AEL_PYTHON`, `python3` and `python3.N` in `/opt/homebrew/bin` and `/usr/local/bin`, virtual environments in `~/.venvs`, `~/.virtualenvs`, `~/venvs`, `~/venv` and `~/.venv`, pyenv, conda, and `/usr/bin/python3`. The first one that runs AEL and has the packages above is used. If none does, the message lists every Python tried and why it failed. No external program is needed: exposure, ISO and capture time come from LibRaw (rawpy) and the EXIF.
- Memory: a run keeps one photo at a time, plus the stack. With 24–42 megapixel cameras, plan for 8–16 GB of free memory and 1–3 minutes per 10 photos.

## Folder

Keep `AELPhotoshop.jsx`, `AELPhotoshopCore.jsx`, `AELPhotoshopDialog.jsx`, `python/` and `runtime/` together. `tools/build_package.py --out NEW_FOLDER --no-demo [--zip FILE]` writes such a folder with the AEL engine of this checkout under `runtime/ael`, checked byte by byte, and `runtime/VERSION.json`.

## Tests

- `tests/run_photoshop_raw.py --plugin photoshop-ael --raw RAWS.txt [--darks DARKS.txt] [--flats FLATS.txt] --output NEW_FOLDER` runs the connector in Photoshop the way a person does: the entry script and the dialog as they are, with the dialog windows, the file and folder choosers, opening a web page and `alert()` replaced by test doubles that press the dialogs' own buttons and keep the messages. In **Advanced settings…** it presses **AEL defaults**, ticks one more filter (by default the NRGF log at 0 %) and presses **OK**; it clicks the GitHub address, whose double keeps the address instead of opening a browser. Photoshop's progress window, the Python run, opening the PSB and Photoshop's own reading of its layers are the real ones. It checks the closing message and its invitation to the library, that the address is written as a blue link with a line under it and the licence line carries the disclaimer on its tip, that every button, tick box, field and link of both windows says what it does, the filter line before and after the advanced settings, that the chosen filters reach the PSB (the extra one hidden) as AEL wrote them and as Photoshop reads them, that the progress said AEL was still working during a filter, that Photoshop's progress window was on screen while AEL worked (the window server is asked every 1.5 s) and changed (two pictures of it, 12 s apart, differ), that exactly one PSB opened with the layers AEL wrote, 16 bits per channel, the PSB's SHA-256 against the receipt, and that the connector's folder is unchanged; and, for the output folder, that the dialog starts at Downloads when no folder is kept, that **Browse…** changes it, that the run goes to a new folder inside the chosen one and that the chosen folder is kept for the next run. The folder kept before the test is put back afterwards, and nothing is written to Downloads. Only documents the test opened are closed. With `--stop-after SECONDS` it stands for a person who presses **Cancel** that long after **Make the image**: AEL must stop in time, say so, leave no Python running and open nothing. The click on Photoshop's own **Cancel** button is not made by the test: it marks the run as cancelled where Photoshop's answer would.
- The older bridge tests (`run_tests.py`, `run_package_tests.py` and the workflow tests) cover the functions the dialog no longer offers: filters, structure and polar from an open document, and the experimental master of `ael_workflow.py`.

## Limitations

- Tested on macOS only. Windows is untested.
- Tried on two eclipses so far (see above).
- One camera and one ISO per run. Each photo is placed by a shift and a turn of the field: optical distortion, which changes as the image drifts across the sensor, is not corrected.
- Without darks, hot pixels stay in the stack, unless the photos of an exposure sit at least 3 px apart on the sensor (see **Dust and hot pixels**).
- The Sun's centre and radius are estimates (see above); the receipt says how each was obtained.
- The connector has not been signed or packaged for automatic updates.
